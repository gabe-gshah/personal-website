**Abstract:** Generative AI (GenAI) has fundamentally reshaped the process of knowledge and creative work. Given the growing adoption of GenAI in communication-centric trades, the current study examines how disclosures of GenAI use influence the persuasiveness of messaging by way of meta-analysis. Relying on theories of persuasive communication, this review summarizes findings from 26 papers (k = 34, N = 8,511) surrounding the effect of AI-disclosure labels on attitudes, intentions, and behaviors – conceptualized together as persuasion. Findings reveal the overall effect of AI-disclosure labels on persuasion is small and non-significant (Hedge’s g = -0.112; p = 0.114). However, subgroup analyses reveal that while AI-disclosure may have null effects on intentions or behavior, this practice does negatively influence attitudes (g = -0.159; p = 0.023). Further, much of this effect can be attributed to the negative effects of AI-disclosure on ad attitudes (g = -0.2; p < 0.000) as opposed to attitudes toward the brand or product (g = 0.057; p = 0.757). These results provide preliminary evidence that AI-disclosures may have fleeting downstream effects, affecting in the moment attitudes but not affecting more complex persuasive outcomes. Additional moderation analyses are required to examine these relationships further.

## Summary
- When considering both behaviors and attitudes, the overall mean effect is small and non-significant. While we coded both attitudes and behaviors, separate analyses shed light on the specifics. Disclosure has a marginally negative effect on behavior, however this relationship was not significant, with the model showing the highest level of heterogeneity out of each of our five models.
- While there is a possibility that moderators, like source credibility, may play a factor in these findings, it is also possible that this null relationship reflects a stopping point for the effect of AI-disclosure on persuasion. That is, AI-disclosures may not have pervasive effects beyond inducing negative ‘in the moment’ attitudes.
- Potential support for this sentiment can be found in our models that examine effects on attitudes, with results showing that attitudes are significantly more negative when AI use is disclosed. However, studies measured various types of attitudes, including attitudes towards the ad, brand, and product. Our results show that the attitudes that are most affected are those toward the ad itself, not those toward the brand or product.
