**Abstract:** We propose a two-part research study. First, given the variety of multimodal LLMs available (both closed and open-source), and the variability in these models’ performance for different tasks (Wang et al., 2024), it is essential to rigorously evaluate a range of models on tasks critical to effective teaming – taskwork and teamwork. To determine the competence of different models we evaluate their performance in tasks relevant to human-AI collaboration (i.e., information retrieval, speaker identification, directive following, planning), using established benchmarks to rank these systems. This project integrates core team‐process theory by mapping team- and taskwork behaviors to established LLM benchmarks. Building on Marks et al. (2001) framework, we specify measurable proxies that capture the ability of LLMs to act as a teammate. By benchmarking models along dimensions laid out by Marks et al. (2001), we create an architecture that can model an “ideal LLM teammate,” combining the best aspects of each model through LLM routing (Schnitzer et al., 2023). Second, using this system we will conduct online, team-oriented experiments, assigning participants into teaming conditions, including (1) human-only teams, (2) human-AI teams with text-only teammates, and (3) human-AI teams with fully multimodal (text, voice, vision) teammates. After random assignment, these teams will complete problem-solving tasks to assess performance and affect (trust and viability) differences between groups. The integration of these approaches will enable this research to construct a holistic understanding of the effects of multimodal LLMs in the workplace and their potential to influence the labor force.
