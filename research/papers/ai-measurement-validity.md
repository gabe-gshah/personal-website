**Abstract:** Studying latent constructs, such as attitude and its strength, has been crucial in advertising theory development. While questionnaires remain one of the most used instruments, text and social media analytics are gaining popularity. Advances in Natural Language Processing and generative AI allow researchers to study consumers’ attitudes with a higher degree of ecological validity, creating an opportunity to access large data sets free of biases attributed to survey research. However, validity of computational measures in the advertising context is generally unknown and best-practices are missing. This study investigates the validity of computational attitude measures and provides researchers with best practices and considerations when applying them. We introduce the working of large language models and generative AI, investigate their precision in detecting attitude towards an ad and its strength, and contrast them with traditional survey measures to assess their convergent validity. Findings show both shortcomings and potential benefits of using computational methods when it comes to accuracy in predicting attitudes, especially as they relate to complexity of the task at hand and reliability. Based on the findings, advertising researchers are provided with dos and don’ts and a research agenda for computational measures of latent constructs such as attitudes.
