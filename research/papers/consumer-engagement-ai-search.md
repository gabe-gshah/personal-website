**Abstract:** The rapid emergence and adoption of AI-powered search engines (AIPSEs; i.e. Google Gemini) present enormous opportunities and challenges for search advertising research and practice. Addressing these opportunities and challenges, this study employs an online observation method, examining consumers’ choice between AIPSEs and traditional search engines (TSEs; i.e., Google), motivations for using each search engine type, and use of these platforms. Results show heavier reliance on TSEs than AIPSEs, more elaborative keyword use on AIPSEs, and heightened use of AIPSEs in advice-seeking circumstances, among other notable findings. These results offer both theoretical and practical implications for the future of search advertising.

## Status
- Published: Journal of Advertising Research

## Summary
- Consumers’ use of AI-powered search engines is increasing, and among those who use both types of search engines, advice-seeking searches are more often conducted on AI-powered search engines than on traditional search engines.
- The impact of AI-powered search engines on search advertising may depend on the type of search, with consumers’ increasing reliance on AI-powered search engines presenting a potentially complementary or competitive implications for traditional search engines.
- Fat-head keyword targeting on traditional search engines would not likely be affected by increasing consumers’ reliance on AI-powered search engines in the near future, but long-tail keyword targeting on traditional search engines would be threatened as consumers divide their elaborative searches across these platforms.
- New tactics devised to disseminate sponsored content on AIPSEs may require focusing more on SEO fine-tuning of websites – or Generative Engine Optimization – as high-quality links seem to be what AIPSEs present to users. This may also require advertisers to investigate the applicability of long-tail or advice-seeking keyword strings with their online offerings, along with alterations to the backend structure of their webpages to allow LLMs to properly read their content.
- Serious consideration should be given to what sponsored ad placement on AIPSEs may look like (i.e., disclosure considerations, positioning on the UI, etc.), and how it will function (i.e., altering LLM text, presenting preconstructed messages), as pay-per-click models may not be the most appropriate model.


## Methods / Data
- We collect observational, digital trace data, highlighting consumers’ natural query and information retrieval processes as they choose to use TSE and AIPSE platforms, identifying how search tactics (i.e., query formulation), motivational differences, and engagement with search results (i.e., clicking behavior) may differ between TSEs and AIPSEs. 
- This study employs an innovative online search behavior observation method, using a web-hosted interface and Google Chrome extension to collect participants' search data.
